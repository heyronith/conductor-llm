Constitutional Control-Plane Transformer (CCPT)

Does giving normative safety computation its own protected internal pathway—isolated from ordinary language capability optimization—produce more robust alignment persistence when a model undergoes continued capability pretraining?

1. Why Safety Persistence Matters

Modern post-training alignment techniques—including Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and supervised safety fine-tuning—typically encode safety behaviors directly into the dense weight matrices that govern general language modeling, reasoning, and factual retrieval. While this paradigm successfully produces compliant behavior in immediate evaluations, it leaves acquired safety constraints vulnerable to downstream distribution shifts and continued learning.

In production settings, language models frequently undergo subsequent rounds of pretraining, continual domain adaptation, or multi-task fine-tuning. During this continued capability training, ordinary cross-entropy loss gradients on unaligned data routinely degrade, overwrite, or bypass previously learned safety behaviors—a vulnerability known as safety persistence collapse or representation erosion.

The working hypothesis of the Constitutional Control-Plane Transformer (CCPT) is that this fragility is fundamentally an architectural consequence of optimization coupling. When normative evaluation and capability generation share identical parameter manifolds, general-purpose gradient steps inevitably drift safety-critical representations. CCPT tests whether establishing strict optimization firewalls—isolating normative computation within a dedicated, gradient-protected internal stream—allows safety mechanisms to persist through substantial capability continuation.

2. The CCPT Dual-Stream Architecture

To decouple safety computation from ordinary language modeling, CCPT organizes internal computation across two asymmetric, parallel pathways:

  • Capability Stream (C, 33.2M parameters): A standard autoregressive causal Transformer backbone that learns vocabulary representations, syntactic structures, factual knowledge, and next-token probability distributions.
  • Normative Stream (N, 2.8M parameters): A dedicated Transformer module tasked exclusively with tracking conversational context, evaluating harmful intent, and computing bounded steering vectors to prevent harmful generation.

Information flow between these streams is strictly asymmetric. The normative stream monitors capability states via a detached observation projection:

N_(l+1) = N_l + F_N(N_l, stop_gradient(C_l))

The stop_gradient operator guarantees that ordinary capability updates cannot backpropagate into or overwrite normative parameter weights.

To intervene during generation, the normative stream applies a gated steering intervention at controlled intermediate layers (Layers 2 and 4):

C_(l+1) = C_l + g_l · ΔC_l + s_l

where ΔC_l is the standard capability layer proposal, g_l ∈ [0, 1] is a bounded scalar gate, and s_l is an additive steering vector parameterized by the normative state.

3. The Controlled Experimental Design

To isolate the causal effect of architectural separation—avoiding confounding variables such as parameter disparities, optimizer differences, or token sequence discrepancies—we compare CCPT (Model C) against a parameter-matched Frozen-Backbone Bottleneck Adapter Control (Model D) across three sequential training stages and three independent random initialization seeds:

  1. Phase 1 (Base Capability Pretraining): Both models train on 1.0 billion tokens of FineWeb-Edu general text using standard autoregressive next-token cross-entropy. Total parameters are strictly matched at ~35.9M.
  2. Phase 2 (Safety Optimization): Capability parameters (θ_C) and backbone parameters are completely frozen. The models train on 20.0 million tokens of WildGuard risk and safe-generation data to acquire refusal behaviors on harmful prompts.
  3. Phase 3 (Persistence Continuation Pretraining): Safety parameters (θ_N and adapters) remain completely frozen. The models undergo 1,000 batches (~2.0 million tokens) of additional capability pretraining on FineWeb-Edu text, testing whether fixed safety mechanisms persist through continued backbone optimization.

All evaluations measure out-of-distribution (OOD) refusal retention on 256 unseen harmful prompts from the BeaverTails benchmark, adjudicated via an authoritative tri-state WildGuard judge (YES / (YES + NO) determinate rate).

4. Empirical Persistence Results & Seed Heterogeneity

Evaluating CCPT against the matched adapter control across three independent initialization seeds reveals a pronounced, but heterogeneous, persistence signal. The primary endpoint (defined as Model C refusal retention minus Model D refusal retention, ΔC - ΔD) produced:

  • Seed 20260821 (Seed 1): Model C retention = -1.17 pp (87.5% → 86.3%) vs. Model D = -42.19 pp (93.4% → 51.2%) → Primary Effect = +41.02 percentage points.
  • Seed 20260823 (Seed 2): Model C retention = -18.36 pp (85.9% → 67.6%) vs. Model D = -4.30 pp (93.0% → 88.7%) → Primary Effect = -14.06 percentage points.
  • Seed 20260824 (Seed 3): Model C retention = +11.72 pp (66.8% → 78.5%) vs. Model D = -10.55 pp (96.1% → 85.5%) → Primary Effect = +22.27 percentage points.

Across the three seeds, the sample mean primary effect is +16.41 pp. However, the substantial sample standard deviation (28.00 pp) and the direction reversal in Seed 2 demonstrate that the architecture does not achieve uniform, seed-invariant persistence.

The Utility Tradeoff: High Benign Over-Refusal

A critical limitation observed across all tested models is high benign over-refusal. On 256 benign prompts from the BeaverTails benchmark, post-persistence over-refusal rates ranged from 64.8% to 79.3% for CCPT and 62.5% to 91.0% for Model D. At this ~36M parameter scale, steering vectors act relatively coarsely: while they reliably suppress harmful completions, they also induce high refusal rates on benign prompts, demonstrating that achieving fine-grained selectivity without over-refusal remains an open optimization challenge.

5. Functional Localization: Controller Causal Ablations

To understand why Seed 2 experienced a persistence decline while Seeds 1 and 3 remained highly resistant to erosion, Task 8 executed test-time causal ablations (evaluating the model with controller_scale = 0.0) and extracted hidden representations across all diagnostic layers.

The ablation results localize Seed 2's failure to a marked reduction in the controller's downstream behavioral efficacy:

  • In Seed 1, the active-vs-off ablation gap expanded from +37.89 pp to +44.38 pp (+6.49 pp change).
  • In Seed 3, the active-vs-off ablation gap expanded from +12.47 pp to +45.60 pp (+33.14 pp change).
  • In Seed 2, the active-vs-off ablation gap collapsed from +43.08 pp to +23.55 pp (-19.53 pp change).

Sensitivity analysis confirms that the direction remains unchanged under both extreme indeterminate (NA) judge treatments.

6. Scientific Assessment: Supported Findings vs. Open Questions

Empirically Supported Findings

  • Active Causal Steering: Test-time ablations prove that the normative stream functions as an active causal controller, creating a 23–45 pp refusal margin over unsteered capability generations.
  • Optimization Isolation: Strict gradient firewalls successfully protect normative parameter weights from cross-entropy updates during LM pretraining.
  • Substantial Persistence Advantage in 2 of 3 Seeds: CCPT achieved major persistence improvements over matched adapters in Seed 1 (+41.0 pp) and Seed 3 (+22.3 pp).

Not Established / Open Research Questions

  • Seed-Invariant Superiority: CCPT did not achieve uniform superiority across all random seeds, suffering a direction reversal in Seed 2 (-14.1 pp).
  • Identified Root Cause for Seed 2: Upstream representation drift metrics (CKA, relative L2) did not monotonically order seed-level outcomes.
  • Acceptable Utility Frontier: High benign over-refusal (>64%) demonstrates that steering selectivity requires further optimization at small scale.
  • Foundation Scale Generalization: Evaluations were conducted on ~36M parameter models; scaling properties on multi-billion parameter models remain unverified.

7. Next Research Directions

Because cross-seed heterogeneity remains partially unexplained and over-refusal is substantial, scaling CCPT to large parameter regimes (such as 10B parameters) is not yet scientifically justified. Immediate research priorities focus on small-scale architectural interface stabilization:

  • Observation Invariant Regularization: Investigating auxiliary loss objectives that regularize capability observation projections against representation drift during continuation training.
  • Adaptive Gating Mechanisms: Exploring norm-bounded, state-dependent gating formulations that preserve controller efficacy across diverse capability trajectories.
  • Selectivity & Utility Tuning: Refining safe generation objectives to reduce over-refusal on benign distributions.
